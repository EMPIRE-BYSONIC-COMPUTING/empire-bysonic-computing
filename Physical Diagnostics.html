Physical Diagnostics

Identifying System Instability: Exposing hidden hardware defects that cause random blue screens (BSODs) or system crashes.

Verify Overclocks: Testing if a manually boosted CPU, GPU, or RAM configuration is safe for daily use.

Thermal Performance: Checking if your CPU or GPU cooling solutions prevent dangerous overheating and thermal throttling.

Benchmarking: The peak performance capabilities of hardware components against industry standards.

Testing Categories & Industry-Standard Tools

CPU (Processor) Stress & Thermals: Computes complex mathematical operations to maximize heat and power draw (Cinebench, Prime95, AIDA64).

GPU (Graphics) 3D Rendering: Simulates heavy gaming workloads to test graphics memory, core stability, and power supplies (3DMark, FurMark, Unigine Heaven).

RAM (Memory) Integrity & Faults: Writes and reads massive data arrays to detect corrupted sectors or timing instability (MemTest86, TestMem5).

Storage (SSD/HDD) Read/Write Speed: Tests sequential and random data transfer limits to assess drive health (CrystalDiskMark, HWiNFO).

Step-by-Step Testing Workflow

[1. Setup Monitoring] ──> [2. Establish Baseline] ──> [3. Run Stress Test] ──> [4. Analyze Stability]

  1. Setup Monitoring Software: Before starting, open a hardware monitoring utility like HWiNFO or MSI Afterburner to track real-time temperatures, voltages, and clock speeds.
  2. Establish a Baseline: Let your PC idle for 5 minutes. Take note of resting temperatures (typically 30°C to 50°C for modern processors).
  3. Run the Test: Launch your chosen tool (e.g., a 10-minute loop of Cinebench). Keep a close eye on your monitoring software.
  4. Analyze Results: Watch out for critical red flags such as thermal throttling (automatic downclocking to protect the chip), visual artifacts (lines or flashing on your screen), or total system lockups.
