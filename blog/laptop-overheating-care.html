Device care

How to keep your laptop cool and spot overheating

Warmth during demanding work can be normal, but persistent excessive heat, repeated shutdowns, or unusual fan noise may point to an airflow or hardware issue. These habits can help you care for your laptop safely.

By Empire Bysonic Computing · Laptop care guide

Laptops produce heat as their processor and other components work. The cooling system moves that heat out through vents, often with the help of a fan. When airflow is restricted or a cooling component is not working properly, the computer may become hot, slow down, or shut itself off to protect its hardware.

Use your laptop where air can circulate

Place the laptop on a firm, flat surface and keep its ventilation openings clear. Soft surfaces such as beds, blankets, or cushions can block vents, while using the device on your lap for long periods may also restrict airflow. If the manufacturer's guidance permits, a stable stand can help keep the base clear.

Avoid placing the computer in direct sunlight or next to a strong heat source. Do not cover it while it is running, and give it room to release warm air.

Keep the surroundings clean and dry

Dust and debris can collect around openings over time. Keep food and drinks away from the laptop and clean its exterior gently with the device powered off, using manufacturer-recommended methods. Avoid inserting objects into vents or applying liquid or household cleaners to openings.

Internal cleaning may require opening the case and handling delicate components. If you are unsure how to do that safely, leave internal cleaning and repairs to a qualified technician rather than forcing a cover or using tools inside the computer.

Notice when the heat occurs

Heavy applications, video calls, games, and updates can make fans run faster. Notice whether the heat settles after the demanding task ends or continues during light use. Also pay attention to recurring fan noise, sudden slowdowns, unexpected shutdowns, or a laptop that becomes unusually hot in the same area each time.

Safety first: If a laptop smells burnt, shows signs of battery swelling, or becomes dangerously hot, stop using it and disconnect power if it is safe. Do not press on, puncture, or attempt to remove a swollen battery; seek professional advice.

When simple habits are not enough

If the laptop continues to overheat on a clear, firm surface, or if performance drops and shutdowns recur, the cause may need diagnosis. Cooling fans, internal dust, thermal components, batteries, and software workload can all play a role. Repeatedly ignoring shutdowns or opening the device without the right experience can risk further damage.

Before arranging an assessment, make a backup if the computer remains stable enough to do so. Write down when the problem happens and any warnings you see. This information can help narrow down the fault.

A quick laptop cooling checklist

  • Use the laptop on a firm surface and keep vents unobstructed.
  • Keep it away from direct sunlight and heat sources.
  • Keep food, drinks, and loose debris away from ventilation openings.
  • Notice recurring fan noise, heat, slowdowns, or shutdowns.
  • Arrange an assessment if the issue persists or gets worse.

Good airflow helps, but it cannot resolve every cooling fault. When overheating keeps returning, an assessment is safer than guessing at the cause.
